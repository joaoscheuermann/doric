# Hooks and the app's lifecycle

- Status: living document. It describes the hook mechanism.

## Summary

A hook is a named point at which the bundle that owns an operation lets other bundles observe it, transform its input, decide whether it goes ahead, or run a step of it. The rule is: **the owner of an operation declares its hooks.** The platform owns the app's lifecycle and declares its hooks in `doric.app.hooks/v1` and `doric.window.hooks/v1`; a bundle declares the hooks of its own operations in a hooks contract of its own, such as `chat.hooks/v2` or `runtime.hooks/v1`. Any bundle may observe a hook, through the contract `hooks/v1`.

A hook is declared with `defineHook`, grouped under a versioned contract id with `defineHooks`, and fired by its owner with `execute` ([`define.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/contracts/define.ts), [`hooks.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/contracts/basic/hooks.ts), [`facades.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/runtime/facades.ts)). A handler is registered with `use` or `whenAvailable` on the same contract ([`hooks.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/contracts/basic/hooks.ts)). The provider of `hooks/v1`, the `core` bundle by default, keeps the handlers, runs them in order and applies one failure rule to all of them ([`core/src/shared/hooks.ts`](../../../bundles/foundation/core/src/shared/hooks.ts)).

## Who declares and who observes

| Role     | What it does                                                                                        | What its manifest lists                                                                                                                                                                    |
| -------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Owner    | Declares a hooks contract, defines it in `hooks/v1` and fires each hook with `execute`              | The contract id with its full version under `provides` (`chat.hooks/v2@2.0.0`), and `hooks/v1@^1.0.0` under `requires`, except `core`, which provides `hooks/v1`                           |
| Observer | Registers handlers on the hooks of a contract it does not own                                       | `hooks/v1@^1.0.0` under `requires`; the hooks contract under `requires` (with a range) or `optional` when it calls `use`, and under `requires` or `optional` when it calls `whenAvailable` |
| Platform | Declares and fires `doric.app.hooks/v1` and `doric.window.hooks/v1`; its principal is named `doric` | Nothing: a bundle does not list a `doric.*` contract                                                                                                                                       |

The owner check is made when the owner defines its contract: the provider refuses a bundle whose manifest does not list the contract id under `provides`, and refuses a `doric.*` contract to anyone but the platform, both with `not_owner` ([`core/src/shared/hooks.ts`](../../../bundles/foundation/core/src/shared/hooks.ts)). `use` on a hooks contract is checked as `services.use` is: it is allowed on a contract the bundle lists under `requires` or `optional`, on a contract it provides, and on a `doric.*` contract, and otherwise throws `undeclared_requirement` ([`activation.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/runtime/activation.ts), [`services.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/runtime/services.ts)). `whenAvailable` is not checked against the manifest ([`facades.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/runtime/facades.ts)); the line under `requires` or `optional` that an observer of the table lists is the convention the shipped bundles follow, not a rule the runtime enforces. A `doric.*` contract needs no line.

## The contract `hooks/v1`

`hooks/v1` is a service contract of cardinality `one` ([`basic/index.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/contracts/basic/index.ts)). The `core` bundle provides it, version 1.0.0, in each of its three environments: `host`, `desktopMain` and `client` ([`core/src/host/index.ts`](../../../bundles/foundation/core/src/host/index.ts), [`core/src/desktop/index.ts`](../../../bundles/foundation/core/src/desktop/index.ts), [`core/src/client/index.ts`](../../../bundles/foundation/core/src/client/index.ts), [`manifest.json`](../../../bundles/foundation/core/manifest.json)).

The full shape of `hooks/v1` and the behavior of the core's provider are in the [core README](../../../bundles/foundation/core/README.md#hooksv1).

A bundle reaches `hooks/v1` with `services.use(HooksV1)`, where `HooksV1` comes from `bundle/contracts`. The value it gets has three members ([`hooks.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/contracts/basic/hooks.ts), [`facades.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/runtime/facades.ts)):

- `define(Contract)`: for the owner. It defines the contract in the provider and returns a handle with one `execute(input, call?)` per hook. The handle is cached per bundle, so a second `define` of the same contract returns the same handle and may be called inside an `execute`.
- `use(Contract)`: for an observer whose manifest lists the contract. It returns one registration function per hook; each takes a handler and returns a `Disposable`.
- `whenAvailable(Contract, callback)`: for an observer that may run without the owner. The callback receives the same registration functions once the owner has defined the contract, and again each time it is defined anew; when the owner's definition goes away, the cleanup the callback returns runs, and the handlers the callback registered synchronously, before its first `await`, are removed. A handler registered after an `await` inside the callback is not tracked: it stays registered when the definition goes away, and the callback registers it again each time the contract is defined anew ([`core/src/shared/hooks.ts`](../../../bundles/foundation/core/src/shared/hooks.ts)). Register synchronously in the callback, or dispose of later registrations in the cleanup. It returns a `Disposable`.

Each of the three members throws `invalid_input` with the message `Expected a hooks contract created with defineHooks.` when its argument is not a contract built by `defineHooks` ([`facades.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/runtime/facades.ts)).

A `whenAvailable` callback waits for a definition in its own generation partition. A callback passed by a call of generation n runs only when the contract is defined by a call of generation n; a kernel callback, generation 0, runs only for a kernel definition. A contract the kernel defines therefore never runs the callbacks of bundles in a host generation process, and a contract a generation defines never runs a kernel callback. Such a bundle registers with `use`, which needs a manifest line for the contract; a kernel `execute` sees those handlers while their generation is active ([`core/src/shared/hooks.ts`](../../../bundles/foundation/core/src/shared/hooks.ts), [`partition.ts`](../../../bundles/foundation/core/src/shared/partition.ts)). A platform contract (`doric.*`) has one partition, so its callbacks always wake.

What each registration lives for ([`core/src/shared/hooks.ts`](../../../bundles/foundation/core/src/shared/hooks.ts), [`link.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/runtime/envelope/link.ts)):

- A handler registered with `use` is kept whether or not the contract is defined, and stays registered when the definition goes away; it runs whenever the owner fires the hook, until its `Disposable` is disposed or its bundle deactivates.
- When a definition goes away, the provider first removes the handlers the callback registered synchronously, then runs the cleanup the callback returned in a microtask. In one process a cleanup that throws is ignored. When the callback runs in another process than the provider, that process disposes of what the callback registered synchronously first and then runs the cleanup; a failure of either is reported to diagnostics as `when_available_failed`.
- Disposing of the `Disposable` that `whenAvailable` returns stops the waiting, removes the callback's tracked handlers and, when the callback ran for the current definition, runs its cleanup in the same way.

What a provider implements is a lower interface, `HooksProvider`, with the methods `define`, `register`, `whenDefined` and `execute`; the runtime builds the three members above over it, so any provider of `hooks/v1` serves the same API ([`hooks.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/contracts/basic/hooks.ts), [`facades.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/runtime/facades.ts)).

A handler receives `(input, call)`. `call.caller` is the bundle that called `execute` (`bundleId`, `version`, `generation`); `call.scope` is a record of strings whose keys belong to the bundles that read them, such as `projectId`, `threadId` and `promptId`, except `windowKey`, the one key the platform reserves and fills for a call that leaves a window; `call.signal` aborts with the operation; `call.trace` is its trace context ([`call.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/contracts/call.ts)); the full shape of the call is in [Services and channels](services-and-channels.md#the-call). An owner passes the call of the operation in progress as the last argument of `execute`, or `{ signal, deadline }`, with the deadline in epoch milliseconds and `0` meaning none. Without either, the dispatcher builds a call whose signal aborts when the caller's generation ends ([`runtime/call.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/runtime/call.ts)).

## Declaring hooks

`defineHook({ kind, input, order?, forOwner? })` declares one hook: its kind and the zod schema of its input. The name of the hook is its key in `defineHooks`, by convention `on<Point>Hook` ([`define.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/contracts/define.ts)).

| Kind        | What each handler returns                       | How the provider runs the handlers                                                                                     | A handler that throws, times out or returns an invalid value                                          |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `observe`   | Nothing                                         | One after another, each awaited within the deadline; `execute` resolves to `undefined`                                 | Reported and skipped; the next handler runs                                                           |
| `transform` | A value of the input schema                     | One after another; each receives the validated output of the one before; `execute` resolves to the last value          | Reported and skipped; the next handler receives the value the skipped one received                    |
| `decide`    | `{ allow: true }` or `{ allow: false, reason }` | One after another; the first refusal ends the chain and is the result; with no refusal the result is `{ allow: true }` | Reported and skipped; it does not count as a refusal                                                  |
| `sequence`  | Nothing                                         | One after another, each awaited with no deadline, in catalog order or, with `order: 'reverse'`, in reverse             | Not reported to diagnostics; the error rejects `execute`, so the owner fails the step the hook guards |

`defineHook` throws a `TypeError` for a kind outside the four (`Invalid hook kind "<kind>".`), for an `order` on a hook that is not `sequence` (`Only a sequence hook takes an order.`) and for a `forOwner` on a hook that is not `observe` (`Only an observe hook takes forOwner.`). `forOwner(input, bundleId)` gives each handler the input meant for that handler's bundle, or `undefined` to skip the handler, and a `forOwner` that throws rejects `execute` ([`define.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/contracts/define.ts), [`core/src/shared/hooks.ts`](../../../bundles/foundation/core/src/shared/hooks.ts)). `forOwner` takes effect only where the provider holds the owner's own definition: a bundle's contract that crosses a process boundary loses it (see [Generations, processes and windows](#generations-processes-and-windows)), so on the host a bundle's `forOwner` holds only for an owner that runs in the kernel (`core`, the foundation, and the resident bundles `host-config`, `transport` and `screen-relay`), and an owner in a generation process loses it.

`defineHooks(id, hooks)` groups hooks under a contract id that matches `contractIdPattern`: lowercase segments separated by `.`, then `/v` and a positive major, so `name/v1` is accepted as well as the conventional `<owner>.<name>/v<major>`; every member must come from `defineHook`, and an id that starts with `doric.` is refused, because those ids belong to the platform, which declares its own with an internal variant that bundles cannot import. Each refusal is a `TypeError` thrown when the contract is declared, with the message `Invalid hooks contract ID "<id>".`, `The hooks contract ID "<id>" is reserved for the platform.` or `Hook "<name>" of "<id>" must be created with defineHook.` ([`define.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/contracts/define.ts), [`ids.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/contracts/ids.ts)). The id carries the major version; the full version is the one the owner lists under `provides`. How a hooks contract takes a minor or a major is in [Versioning](versioning.md).

The owner exports the contract from its `contracts` entry point (for example `bundles/desktop/chat/src/contracts/index.ts`), and an observer that depends on the owner imports it from `<owner>/contracts` ([`mcp/src/host/index.ts`](../../../bundles/features/mcp/src/host/index.ts)). The provider matches a registration to a definition by contract id and hook name only ([`core/src/shared/hooks.ts`](../../../bundles/foundation/core/src/shared/hooks.ts)), so an observer may declare a structural copy of the hooks it observes, with the same id, names, kinds and inputs, in place of the import. In one process the input its handler gets is the value the owner's definition validated; across processes each side validates with its own schema, as [Generations, processes and windows](#generations-processes-and-windows) describes. `files`, `git`, `notifications` and `openui` keep a copy of `chat.hooks/v2`, and `chat` a copy of `workspace.hooks/v1`, so that they load in a composition without the owner, where `<owner>/contracts` is not mapped; they list the contract under `optional` ([`files/src/client/api/peers.ts`](../../../bundles/features/files/src/client/api/peers.ts), [`git/src/client/api/peers.ts`](../../../bundles/features/git/src/client/api/peers.ts), [`notifications/src/client/observer.ts`](../../../bundles/desktop/notifications/src/client/observer.ts), [`openui/src/client/chat-contract.ts`](../../../bundles/features/openui/src/client/chat-contract.ts), [`chat/src/client/api/workspace-hooks.ts`](../../../bundles/desktop/chat/src/client/api/workspace-hooks.ts)). `runtime` keeps a copy of `core.event-hooks/v1`, so that its package takes no dependency on the core's package ([`runtime/src/host/peers.ts`](../../../bundles/runtime/runtime/src/host/peers.ts), [`runtime/package.json`](../../../bundles/runtime/runtime/package.json)). `terminal`'s host part keeps a copy of `runtime.hooks/v1`, with the other runtime contracts it calls, although the `terminal` package lists `runtime` under `dependencies` and its client part imports `runtime/contracts` ([`terminal/src/host/peers.ts`](../../../bundles/features/terminal/src/host/peers.ts), [`terminal/src/client/peers.ts`](../../../bundles/features/terminal/src/client/peers.ts), [`terminal/package.json`](../../../bundles/features/terminal/package.json)). `runtime` and `terminal` list the contract they copy under `requires`.

## Firing a hook

The owner defines its contract and fires a hook:

```ts
const hooks = services.use(HooksV1).define(RuntimeHooksV1);

await hooks.onThreadsClosingHook.execute({ projectId, threadIds }, call);
```

On `define`, the provider checks the owner as described above and records it; a second bundle that defines the same id in the same generation gets `registration_conflict`, with the message `<id> is already defined by "<owner>".`, and a second `define` by the same owner changes nothing. A kernel definition and a generation definition of the same id do not collide. Then `define` runs the waiting `whenAvailable` callbacks of its partition synchronously, in the order they were passed. In one process a callback that throws makes `define` throw its error after the contract is already defined, and the callbacks after it do not run; a callback that throws inside `whenAvailable`, when the contract is already defined, makes `whenAvailable` throw; the callback stays registered, cannot be disposed, since no `Disposable` is returned, and runs again at each later definition. A callback in another process than the provider runs there without blocking `define`, and its failure is reported as `when_available_failed`. The handle has no `dispose`: the definition is tracked in the owner's scope and goes away when the owner deactivates, which undefines the contract and removes what the `whenAvailable` callbacks registered ([`dispatch.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/runtime/dispatch.ts), [`facades.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/runtime/facades.ts), [`core/src/shared/hooks.ts`](../../../bundles/foundation/core/src/shared/hooks.ts), [`link.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/runtime/envelope/link.ts)).

A `not_owner` or `registration_conflict` refusal is a registration error of the defining bundle ([Services and channels](services-and-channels.md#errors), [`dispatch.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/runtime/dispatch.ts)). In one process it is thrown by `define`; when it escapes the bundle's `activate`, the bundle is quarantined with `registration_failed`, and a refusal the bundle catches quarantines nothing ([`activation.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/runtime/activation.ts)). In a host generation process `define`, `use` registrations and `whenAvailable` cross to the kernel's provider and return at once; the refusal arrives later. During activation, the runtime waits for every such call of the bundle after `activate` and quarantines the bundle with `registration_failed` on the first refusal; after activation the refusal is reported to diagnostics as `registration_failed` with the bundle's id, and the handle `define` returned stays cached and fires the hooks ([`values.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/runtime/envelope/values.ts), [`link.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/runtime/envelope/link.ts), [`facades.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/runtime/facades.ts)).

Only `define` checks the owner. `execute` checks neither the owner nor whether the contract is defined: it runs the registered handlers for whatever contract it is given and validates the input against that contract's schema ([`core/src/shared/hooks.ts`](../../../bundles/foundation/core/src/shared/hooks.ts)). The desktop main process uses this: it executes `doric.app.hooks/v1` in the desktop generation's `hooks/v1` without defining it in main ([`process.ts`](../../../app/desktop/doric/src/platform/generation/process.ts)).

On `execute`, the provider of the core ([`core/src/shared/hooks.ts`](../../../bundles/foundation/core/src/shared/hooks.ts)):

1. refuses a hook name the contract lacks (`<id> has no hook "<hook>".`) and an input that fails the schema (`Invalid input for <id> <hook>: …`, with the zod `issues` in `details`), with `invalid_input`;
2. takes the handlers registered for that contract id and hook that the call's generation sees, and, for `doric.window.hooks/v1`, only those registered for the call's `scope.windowKey`;
3. sorts them by the activation order of their bundles in the catalog, the platform's own first, and by registration order within one bundle;
4. runs them by the rule of the hook's kind (table above). A handler of a hook that is not `sequence` has 10,000 ms (`DEFAULT_HOOK_DEADLINE_MS`), with two exceptions: `onCloseHook` has no deadline, and `onFlushHook` has the time left before the deadline of the call that fired it, measured when `execute` starts, or no deadline when the call has none or none is left;
5. for a hook that is not `sequence`, reports a handler that throws, returns an invalid value or passes its deadline to [`doric.diagnostics/v1`](observability.md#diagnostics-doricdiagnosticsv1), as `hook_handler_failed` or `hook_handler_timeout`. The report's `bundleId` is the failing handler's bundle, its `details` are `{ contract, hook }`, and its message is the thrown error's message, `<hook> handler did not settle within <ms> ms.`, `<hook> handler returned an invalid value: …` (a `transform` result that fails the input schema) or `<hook> handler returned an invalid decision: …` (a `decide` result that is not a decision). A report that fails is ignored. A handler that passes its deadline is not stopped: the chain moves on and the handler's late result is dropped;
6. rejects `execute` with the call's abort reason when `call.signal` aborts first, or at once when it has already aborted. The chain is not stopped: it starts even with an aborted signal, and the handler that runs and the ones after it keep running after `execute` rejects; they see the abort through `call.signal`.

Activation order puts the foundation first, then `core`, then the other bundles, each group in dependency order with ties broken by bundle id; within the foundation, the providers of `storage.backend/v1` and `secrets.backend/v1` come first ([`catalog.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/runtime/catalog.ts)). The **foundation**, in the catalog's sense, is the set of bundles that the composition chooses under `providers` for a contract the core provides or requires in that environment. In the shipped host composition it is `postgres`, chosen for `storage.backend/v1` and `secrets.backend/v1`, which the core requires ([`composition.json`](../../../app/backend/doric-host/composition.json)). The term is not the folder `bundles/foundation/`: `host-config` and `transport` are in that folder but are not chosen providers, so they activate after the core.

When an owner fires a hook without awaiting it, it ignores the result: `runtime` fires `onProjectReadyHook` and `onProjectClosingHook` after the commit and drops any rejection, and `core` fires `onEventAppendedHook` after an append in the same way ([`runtime/src/host/ports.ts`](../../../bundles/runtime/runtime/src/host/ports.ts), [`core/src/host/events/facade.ts`](../../../bundles/foundation/core/src/host/events/facade.ts)).

## Registering a handler

An observer registers in the `activate` of its part:

```ts
const closing = services.use(HooksV1).whenAvailable(RuntimeHooksV1, (hooks) => {
  const registration = hooks.onThreadsClosingHook(host.threadsClosing);
  return () => registration.dispose();
});
```

This is how `terminal` registers its cleanup of a Thread's terminals ([`terminal/src/host/index.ts`](../../../bundles/features/terminal/src/host/index.ts)). A `Disposable` that a call to `hooks/v1` returns is tracked in the calling bundle's scope ([`dispatch.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/runtime/dispatch.ts)), so the registrations of a bundle are removed when the bundle deactivates, after its `onDeactivateHook` and before its cleanup runs ([`activation.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/runtime/activation.ts)). A part that registers for a shorter time, such as a mounted page, disposes of the registration itself ([`use-config-autosave.ts`](../../../bundles/desktop/settings/src/client/hooks/use-config-autosave.ts)).

## Generations, processes and windows

The host runs its bundles in generation processes, and its `hooks/v1` is the one in the kernel; a hooks contract crosses the process boundary as its id and, for each hook, its name, its `kind`, the JSON Schema of its input and its `order`, and the receiving side rebuilds it from those, except the two platform contracts, which it takes as they are ([`values.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/runtime/envelope/values.ts)). `forOwner` is not carried: when an owner in a generation process defines its contract in the kernel's provider, the rebuilt contract has no `forOwner`, and every handler receives the whole input. Across the boundary each side validates with its own schema ([`values.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/runtime/envelope/values.ts)):

- the owner's `execute` parses the input with the owner's schema before it is sent, or sends it as given when that parse fails, and the kernel's provider validates it again against the schema rebuilt from the JSON Schema, which keeps neither zod refinements nor transforms, and refuses it with `invalid_input` when it fails that schema;
- a handler in a generation process receives the value parsed with its own bundle's schema of the hook, or the value as it arrived when that parse fails;
- the result of a `transform` hook is parsed again with the owner's schema when it comes back, and kept as it arrived when that parse fails.

The provider keeps each registration with the generation of the call that made it: a registration made in generation 0, the kernel, is seen by every execution; one made in a generation is seen only by executions in that generation, or by kernel executions while that generation is active ([`partition.ts`](../../../bundles/foundation/core/src/shared/partition.ts)). A bundle hooks contract has one definition per generation, so the same owner in two generations does not conflict, and a kernel definition does not conflict with a generation definition of the same id; a `doric.*` contract has one definition. The `whenAvailable` callbacks follow the same partitions, so a callback of a generation process waits for a definition made in that generation, as [The contract `hooks/v1`](#the-contract-hooksv1) describes ([`core/src/shared/hooks.ts`](../../../bundles/foundation/core/src/shared/hooks.ts)). How generations are started and drained is in [Process isolation](process-isolation.md).

In the client, each window has its own `hooks/v1`, and the provider runs a `doric.window.hooks/v1` handler only for the window whose `scope.windowKey` registered it ([`core/src/shared/hooks.ts`](../../../bundles/foundation/core/src/shared/hooks.ts)).

## Hooks the platform declares

`doric.app.hooks/v1` (`AppHooksV1`) and `doric.window.hooks/v1` (`WindowHooksV1`) are exported by `bundle/contracts` ([`platform.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/contracts/platform.ts)). A bundle registers on them as on any other hooks contract, with `services.use(HooksV1).use(AppHooksV1)`; no hook names a bundle or a bundle's contract, and each bundle decides what a hook means to it.

### When the platform defines them

In an environment whose `hooks/v1` is provided in the same process, the platform defines `doric.app.hooks/v1` as soon as the provider has activated. The bundles activated before it, the foundation and the provider itself, are not observed: no `onActivateHook` or `onDeactivateHook` fires for them. In a host generation process, `hooks/v1` is the kernel's, so the platform defines the contract before the first bundle activates, and every bundle is observed ([`activation.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/runtime/activation.ts), [`runner.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/runtime/runner.ts)). When the platform's `define` of `doric.app.hooks/v1` throws, the failure is reported to diagnostics as `app_hooks_failed` with the platform's id, activation goes on, and no `onActivateHook` or `onDeactivateHook` fires in that environment ([`activation.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/runtime/activation.ts)). In a client window, the platform defines `doric.window.hooks/v1` once the window's `hooks/v1` provider has activated. The same code reports a failure of that step as `app_hooks_failed`; `doric.app.hooks/v1` is then already defined, so `onActivateHook` and `onDeactivateHook` still fire, and the window has no window hooks ([`packages/desktop/src/client/activate.ts`](../../../packages/desktop/src/client/activate.ts)).

Desktop: when a new desktop generation has loaded, fired into that generation ([`manager.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/process/manager.ts), [`app.ts`](../../../app/desktop/doric/src/platform/app.ts))                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| `onAppReadyHook`          | `observe`  | `{}`                     | `desktopMain`: once the desktop bundles have activated at start; not again for a desktop generation process respawned after a loss ([`process.ts`](../../../app/desktop/doric/src/platform/generation/process.ts), [`app.ts`](../../../app/desktop/doric/src/platform/app.ts))                                                                                                                                                                            |
| `onAppActivateHook`       | `observe`  | `{}`                     | `desktopMain`: when the app is activated, as by a click on its dock icon ([`lifecycle.ts`](../../../app/desktop/doric/src/platform/lifecycle.ts))                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| `onBeforeQuitHook`        | `decide`   | `{}`                     | `desktopMain`: on a request to quit; a refusal keeps the app open and logs `Quit denied` with `{ reason }`, a second request while the first waits quits at once, and a failure or a result that is not a decision counts as allow. On allow, main saves the window paths and its state before it quits, and logs `Desktop state could not be saved before quit` when that fails ([`lifecycle.ts`](../../../app/desktop/doric/src/platform/lifecycle.ts)) |
| `onConnectionChangedHook` | `observe`  | `{ connected: boolean }` | `desktopMain`: when the connection to the host drops or returns; a repeated value is not fired ([`lifecycle.ts`](../../../app/desktop/doric/src/platform/lifecycle.ts))                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |

Every input is a strict zod object, so `execute` refuses an extra key with `invalid_input`. The named inputs are ([`platform.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/contracts/platform.ts)):

```ts
type Activation = {
  bundleId: string;
  version: string;
  generation: number; // integer
  environment: 'host' | 'desktopMain' | 'client';
};
type Owner = { generation: number; incarnation: number }; // integers
type Live = Owner & {
  state: 'starting' | 'active' | 'draining' | 'stopped' | 'failed';
};
type GenerationChange = {
  event: 'activated' | 'draining' | 'lost' | 'stopped';
  generation: number;
  incarnation: number;
  self: Owner; // the receiving generation process
  live: Live[]; // the live generations after the change
  holds?: { bundleId: string; key: string; label?: string }[]; // `lost` only
};
type PeerSignal = {
  topic: string; // 1 to 128 characters
  payload: unknown;
  from: Owner & { bundleId: string };
  self: Owner;
  live: Live[];
};
```

The kernel executes `onGenerationChangedHook` and `onPeerSignalHook` once per receiving generation, concurrently, each with a call pinned to that generation, so the handlers of that generation and of the kernel run; it fires neither while it stops ([`manager.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/process/manager.ts), [Process isolation](process-isolation.md#generation-hooks)).

The desktop main process fires its hooks into the newest desktop generation process, and `onGenerationReadyHook` into the generation that loaded, through that generation's `hooks/v1` ([`app.ts`](../../../app/desktop/doric/src/platform/app.ts)); with no provider there, a `decide` hook allows and the others do nothing ([`process.ts`](../../../app/desktop/doric/src/platform/generation/process.ts)). A failure of the platform's own `execute` does not stop the lifecycle step, except for `onHostStartHook`, and is recorded by the process that fired the hook:

- the activation loop of every environment reports a failed `onActivateHook` or `onDeactivateHook` to diagnostics as `hook_execute_failed` ([`activation.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/runtime/activation.ts));
- the host kernel reports a failed `onHostStopHook`, `onHostReadyHook`, `onGenerationReadyHook`, `onGenerationChangedHook` or `onPeerSignalHook` to diagnostics as `hook_execute_failed`, and a failed `onHostStartHook` stops the host ([`kernel.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/process/kernel.ts), [`manager.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/process/manager.ts));
- the desktop main process does not report to diagnostics: it logs `App hook failed` for `onAppActivateHook`, `onBeforeQuitHook` and `onConnectionChangedHook`, `onAppReadyHook failed` and `onGenerationReadyHook failed` for those two hooks ([`lifecycle.ts`](../../../app/desktop/doric/src/platform/lifecycle.ts), [`distribution.ts`](../../../app/desktop/doric/src/platform/catalog/distribution.ts), [`app.ts`](../../../app/desktop/doric/src/platform/app.ts)).

In recovery mode the host fires none of its three host hooks ([`kernel.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/process/kernel.ts)).

### `doric.window.hooks/v1`

| Hook          | Kind      | Input | Result      | When each window fires it                                                                                                                         |
| ------------- | --------- | ----- | ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `onCloseHook` | `decide`  | `{}`  | `Decision`  | The main process asks the window to close; a refusal keeps it open, with its reason. A forced close skips the hook. No deadline                   |
| `onFlushHook` | `observe` | `{}`  | `undefined` | After the close is allowed: the window saves what is pending, within the deadline the main process sets for the flush and passes with the request |

Both are answered by the window's close protocol, which runs in two phases ([`close.ts`](../../../packages/desktop/src/client/close.ts), [`windows/close.ts`](../../../app/desktop/doric/src/platform/windows/close.ts), [`native-windows.ts`](../../../app/desktop/doric/src/platform/windows/native-windows.ts)):

1. **Veto.** Main holds the window's close and asks the renderer, with no deadline. The renderer allows at once when the request is forced or the window has no `hooks/v1` provider, and otherwise answers with the result of `onCloseHook`. A refusal leaves the window open and main logs `Window close denied` with `{ windowKey, reason }`. A second close while one is pending, or any close while the app quits, is forced. A renderer that never answers stays open until it is closed again.
2. **Flush.** After an allow, main asks the renderer to flush with a deadline of `WINDOW_FLUSH_DEADLINE_MS`, 1,500 ms from now, in epoch milliseconds, and closes the window when the renderer answers or the deadline passes, whichever comes first. The renderer runs `onFlushHook` with that deadline and ignores its failure.

How windows are opened and closed is in [Windows](windows.md).

## Host start and stop

Activation opens no database, so the work a host needs before it serves runs in `onHostStartHook`. Each handler names the stage it runs with `doric.startup/v1.stage(name)` and writes its log lines with `doric.startup/v1.log(record)`, so a failure is logged at that stage; the contract is described in [Observability](observability.md#the-hosts-startup-record-doricstartupv1). When a start step fails, the error rejects the host's start: the host logs one fatal line, `Doric failed to start`, with `{ stage }` naming the last stage set, and exits with code 1 ([`platform.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/contracts/platform.ts), [`index.ts`](../../../app/backend/doric-host/src/index.ts)). The host names its own stages: `bootstrap`, `bundle_load`, `network_binding` and `ready`. The shipped bundles name these:

| Stage                      | Bundle                  | What its start step does                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `database_client`          | `postgres`              | Connects the process's database client, so a missing URL fails here; an unreachable database fails at the first query, in the `credential_activation` stage. `postgres` registers no hook of its own: the core's start step calls `storage.backend/v1.connect`, which runs this step ([`postgres/src/host/start.ts`](../../../bundles/foundation/postgres/src/host/start.ts), [`core/src/host/secrets/activation.ts`](../../../bundles/foundation/core/src/host/secrets/activation.ts)) |
| `credential_activation`    | `core`                  | Reads the stored credentials once through `secrets/v1`, so a missing or unusable credential key fails here ([`activation.ts`](../../../bundles/foundation/core/src/host/secrets/activation.ts))                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| `configuration_activation` | `host-config`           | Reads the stored configuration and builds its providers ([`configuration.ts`](../../../bundles/foundation/host-config/src/host/configuration.ts))                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| `sandbox_pool`             | `docker`, `firecracker` | Reports the sandbox pool's settings; the pool is built at activation ([`docker/src/host/start.ts`](../../../bundles/sandboxes/docker/src/host/start.ts), [`firecracker/src/host/start.ts`](../../../bundles/sandboxes/firecracker/src/host/start.ts), [`docker/src/host/sandbox.ts`](../../../bundles/sandboxes/docker/src/host/sandbox.ts), [`firecracker/src/host/sandbox.ts`](../../../bundles/sandboxes/firecracker/src/host/sandbox.ts))                                           |
| `workspace_reconciliation` | `runtime`               | Reconciles the persisted Projects and Threads and logs what it interrupted ([`lifecycle.ts`](../../../bundles/runtime/runtime/src/host/lifecycle.ts))                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |

`transport` also has a start step, which names no stage and logs the Socket.IO namespaces it serves ([`transport/src/kernel.ts`](../../../bundles/foundation/transport/src/kernel.ts)).

At shutdown, on the first `SIGINT` or `SIGTERM`, the host closes the HTTP server and the sockets and runs `onHostStopHook`, in which `runtime` suspends its Projects, ending their running prompts, while the other bundles are still active ([`index.ts`](../../../app/backend/doric-host/src/index.ts), [`lifecycle.ts`](../../../bundles/runtime/runtime/src/host/lifecycle.ts), [`service.ts`](../../../bundles/runtime/runtime/src/host/domain/service.ts)). Then the kernel stops: the generation processes stop first, and their bundles deactivate, which disposes of the sandbox pool; then the kernel's own bundles deactivate in reverse activation order, which disconnects the database ([`kernel.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/process/kernel.ts), [`docker/src/host/main.ts`](../../../bundles/sandboxes/docker/src/host/main.ts), [`postgres/src/host/index.ts`](../../../bundles/foundation/postgres/src/host/index.ts)).

### The platform's hooks in shipped bundles

- `core` sets the menu skeleton (the top-level menus), migrates the stored selection, and then opens the workspace window in `onAppReadyHook`, and focuses or reopens that window in `onAppActivateHook` ([`core/src/desktop/index.ts`](../../../bundles/foundation/core/src/desktop/index.ts)). The core owns the menu skeleton, and the shipped bundles add items and keybindings through the core's `menus/v1`, which records the calling bundle as the item's owner before it passes the item to `doric.menus/v1`. The runtime does not make that path exclusive: `doric.*` contracts need no manifest line, and the desktop's `doric.menus/v1` takes `owner` as the caller sends it ([`services.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/runtime/services.ts), [`broker.ts`](../../../app/desktop/doric/src/platform/generation/broker.ts), [`menu.ts`](../../../bundles/foundation/core/src/desktop/menu.ts), [`menus.ts`](../../../bundles/foundation/core/src/desktop/menus.ts)).
- `history` flushes its store and allows in `onBeforeQuitHook` ([`history/src/desktop/index.ts`](../../../bundles/desktop/history/src/desktop/index.ts)).
- `settings` saves a pending change of its page in `onFlushHook` ([`use-config-autosave.ts`](../../../bundles/desktop/settings/src/client/hooks/use-config-autosave.ts)).
- `runtime` hands Projects over between generations: in `onGenerationChangedHook` the active generation recovers and a draining one releases its Projects as their prompts end, and on `lost` or `stopped` of another generation the active runtime adopts that generation's Projects; each release is signalled with `doric.generation/v1.signal`, and the active runtime adopts the released Projects in `onPeerSignalHook` ([`handover.ts`](../../../bundles/runtime/runtime/src/host/handover.ts)).
- The start steps of `onHostStartHook` and the stop step of `runtime` are in [Host start and stop](#host-start-and-stop).

## Replacing the provider of `hooks/v1`

`hooks/v1` has no `doric.` prefix, so another bundle may provide it. Because its cardinality is `one`, a composition that enables a second provider chooses it under `providers`, for example `"providers": { "hooks/v1": "myhooks" }`; without that choice the second provider is quarantined with `unchosen_provider` and the core stays the provider ([`catalog.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/runtime/catalog.ts)). A chosen provider of a contract the core provides becomes part of the foundation (defined in [Firing a hook](#firing-a-hook)), which activates before the core. When a chosen foundation provider is quarantined, the catalog does not fall back to the core: the environment starts in recovery mode ([`catalog.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/runtime/catalog.ts)). A foundation bundle that lists a contract under `requires` is quarantined with `foundation_requires`, except a replacement of `events/v1`, `settings/v1` or `models/v1` that requires a storage or secrets backend ([`catalog.ts`](../../../packages/bundle/src/runtime/catalog.ts)). The chosen provider receives every `define`, `register`, `whenDefined` and `execute`, the platform's included, so the order, the deadline, the validation and the failure rule described here are what any provider must implement; `doric.app.hooks/v1` and `doric.window.hooks/v1` do not change with it. How a composition chooses providers is in [Composition and distribution](composition-and-distribution.md).
